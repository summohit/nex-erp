1. Where we are today, and problems to fix

In MIRA's code

  • Hardcoded tax and statutory rules in the monthly run: PF 12% of Basic, ESI 0.75% at or below ₹21,000 gross, Professional Tax a flat ₹200 above ₹15,000, and TDS = 10% of anything above ₹50,000 a month, which is not how income tax works. Finance cannot change any of these.
  • Salary split settings live in three places (salary template, payroll settings, offer-letter settings). An offer annexure and a payslip can disagree for the same CTC.
  • Components are only a name + Earning/Deduction. No value, basis, conditions, taxable or loss-of-pay flags. Each employee's amounts are typed by hand.
  • Missing: salary groups, tax regimes, slab tables, consultant TDS/GST, effective dates, salary revisions, and an audit log of setting changes.
  • Grades use monthly gross; the MOM says grades are CTC-based.

2. Design principles

  1. Everything is data. Every rate, slab, component, group, formula and dropdown is edited on a screen.
  2. One calculation engine. Offer annexure, onboarding, payroll preview, payslips and the calculator all call the same service.
  3. Effective dates. A change applies from its date. Finalized payslips never change: they keep a copy of amounts and the rule version used.
  4. Safe editing. Formulas are checked on save; a "try it" calculator shows the result before saving; nothing used on a payslip is deleted, only deactivated.
  5. Audit everything. Who changed what, when, old value and new value.
  6. Permissions. Finance edits payroll masters, HR views, the super admin has everything.

3. Old and new tax regimes

Each employee chooses a regime for each financial year. New is the default if they don't choose. MIRA stores both regimes as editable tables per financial year, and the engine works out monthly TDS from whichever one the employee picked.

How MIRA will handle regimes

  1. Settings, per financial year: a regime record with an editable slab table, standard deduction, rebate limit and maximum, marginal relief on/off, cess %, surcharge table and age-based nil limits. Next year's figures are added as a new version; last year's stay for history.
  2. Deduction sections master (old regime): code, name, yearly limit, proof required, which regime it applies to. All editable as section numbers change under the new Act.
  3. Employee choice: at the start of the year (or when joining), the employee picks a regime in their profile with a side-by-side comparison of their own estimated tax under both. Finance can lock the choice after a set date.
  4. Declarations: in April the employee declares planned investments (old regime). In January–February they upload proofs; Finance approves or reduces amounts; TDS for the remaining months is corrected.
  5. Monthly TDS = (projected yearly tax − TDS already deducted this year) ÷ months left. This spreads tax evenly and absorbs bonuses, revisions and arrears.
  6. Mid-year joiners enter income and TDS from their previous employer (Form 12B) so the projection is right.
  7. Year end: a TDS summary per employee for Form 24Q and Form 16.
Rates above are the FY 2025-26 rules carried into FY 2026-27 as far as we know. Finance/CA must confirm before go-live, and enter them in the settings screen. No code change needed when they differ.

4. The plan, phase by phase

Phase 1 · Master data every list editable

  • Salary components: name, code (e.g. BASIC, HRA), type (Earning / Deduction / Employer contribution / Info), value type (Fixed, % of CTC, % of Basic, % of Gross, Formula, Balance, Slab table, Entered monthly), cap on base (PF on Basic up to ₹15,000), condition (ESI only if gross ≤ ₹21,000), rounding, min/max, flags (part of CTC, taxable, reduced for LOP, on payslip, on annexure), order, active, effective from.
  • Salary groups: name, pay type (Employee / Trainee / Consultant), components with per-group values, PF yes/no, ESI yes/no, tax method (regime slabs / flat % / none), GST yes/no and rate. The old tool's 7 groups move across, corrected.
  • Tax regimes and deduction sections (section 3).
  • Statutory rates: PF (employee %, employer %, EPS split, wage ceiling, admin charges), ESI (employee %, employer %, limit), Professional Tax slab table per state, LWF.
  • Contractor TDS and GST: sections with rate and threshold; GST rates.
  • Grades: annual CTC bands, km rate, costing rate.
  • Other dropdowns: payment methods, pay frequency, contract lengths (6 m / 1 y / 2 y), worker types, states, currency, working days and hours for costing, payslip and annexure wording.

Phase 2 · One calculation engine and a salary calculator

  • Input: CTC (or monthly gross), salary group, date, regime. Output: monthly and yearly breakdown, employer cost, net pay, estimated tax under both regimes.
  • Formulas use component codes and are validated on save (no loops, no unknown codes).
  • Calculator screen for Finance to compare with their Excel. Tests built from Finance's Excel examples.
  • Replaces the hardcoded rules and merges the three old settings sources.

Phase 3 · Employee pay profile

  • Salary group, annual CTC, effective date, regime, PAN, UAN/PF no., ESI no., bank details, payment method, component overrides with reason.
  • Salary revisions with history; back-dated revisions add arrears to the next payslip. Grade set from CTC.

Phase 4 · Recruitment → offer → onboarding MOM P0/P1

  • Offer: annual CTC + salary group → engine → annexure. On acceptance the annexure is locked as version 1. On joining the pay profile is created from it. Later changes go through an approved revision (and re-acceptance where needed).

Phase 5 · Monthly payroll run

  • Lock attendance → monthly inputs (bonus, arrears, one-off deductions, approved expenses, loan recovery, leave encashment) → preview with warnings → calculate (LOP only on flagged components; TDS by projection) → approve → finalize (frozen) → mark paid → payslips by email/PDF.
  • Reports: salary register, bank transfer file, PF ECR, ESI return, PT, TDS summary (24Q / Form 16), employer cost, department totals.

Phase 6 · Consultants and trainees

  • Consultants: monthly fee, TDS at section rate, GST added when registered; payout statement. Trainees: fixed stipend, reduced only for unpaid leave.
  • Proposed control (legal review): once PF appears on a finalized payslip, moving to a no-PF/consultant group needs approval.

Phase 7 · Costing and extras

  • Hourly/daily cost = (CTC + employer contributions) ÷ (working days × hours), all editable; feeds project costing.
  • Later: reverse calculation (target net → CTC), loans, Form 16 generation.

Questions for Finance (before step 2)

  1. FY 2026-27 slabs, standard deduction, rebate, cess and surcharge for both regimes (CA to confirm under the new Income-tax Act).
  2. Default regime for employees who don't choose, and the date the choice locks.
  3. ESI base (gross or Basic), and whether "Variable Deduction 3.25%" is really the employer's ESI share.
  4. PF on capped Basic (₹15,000) for everyone, or full Basic for some?
  5. Professional Tax: which states do employees work in? (UP has none.)
  6. Consultant TDS base (fee or CTC) and the missing TDS on "Non-Technical Consultant with GST".
  7. What "Other Deduction ₹1,800" is.
  8. Grade CTC bands; costing days and hours, and what goes into cost.
  9. Who approves salary revisions and the monthly run (Finance head, super admin, both?).
Nothing in this document changes how anyone is paid today. Current payroll keeps running until step 2 switches it to the new engine, after Finance has checked the calculator against their Excel.
