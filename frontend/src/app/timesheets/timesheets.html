The three hour figures

Login — how long the person was clocked in, from Attendance.

Logged — how much of that they booked to a task.

Unlogged — the gap between the two. This is the number the screen exists for.

Marks on a day

unstopped timer? One entry runs over 12 hours — almost certainly a timer nobody stopped. Common in time migrated from Workway.

no attendance Time booked on a day with no clock-in at all.

over-booked More hours booked than the person was clocked in for.

still clocked in The day is still open. Login hours stay empty until clock-out, so a running day does not climb through the afternoon on its own.

auto-closed Nobody clocked out; the 23:00 sweep closed the day. The login hours are a cutoff, not an observation.

edited since The logs changed after the day was submitted for approval.

! In the team views, this day carries one of the marks above.

Approval (§22)

Not submitted Nothing sent for review yet.

Pending Waiting on a project manager or admin.

Approved Counted as reviewed time.

Rejected Sent back with a reason; the employee re-submits.

Entries

Timer Recorded by starting and stopping the timer.

Manual Typed in by hand, or migrated from Workway.

Inactive The user account is suspended — in practice, someone who has left. Their hours stay because the history is real.

A project, client, manager or entry-type filter is on, so logged hours cover only the matching entries. Unlogged hours are hidden — they only mean something against a whole day.
